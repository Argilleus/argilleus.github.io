About the Work

Pavane pour une infante défunte was composed by Maurice Ravel in 1899 for solo piano while he was a student at the Paris Conservatoire. Despite its title, Ravel explained that the work was not intended as a lament for a deceased princess, but rather as an evocation of the slow, stately court dance that a young Spanish infanta might once have danced. With its elegant melody, refined harmonies, and restrained melancholy, the piece has become one of Ravel's best-known compositions and remains a staple of the piano repertoire.
